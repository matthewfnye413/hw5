3. What would have been simpler as one agent with tools, and why

  • Ticket 102, clearly. It’s a straight line: read the lease, check cash, request payment. One agent with get_lease, get_cash_balance and request_payment_approval does it in about 4 tool calls and 2–3 model requests. The team used 10 requests and 19.8k tokens because each hand-off re-sends a prompt, a task and a report.
    Evidence: Ticket 102 run bar: 19,798 tokens, 10 requests, 7 tool calls
  • Ticket 101, mostly. Stock check, vendor check, invoice check, approval request, draft. All of it is sequential and nothing benefits from a second “opinion.” The duplicate Inventory lookups only happened because there were multiple agents asking.
    Evidence: Ticket 101: check_stock + list_vendors run twice
  • Ticket 103’s fact-gathering (stock, vendor, pricing, cash). The 7 hand-offs were mostly agents re-asking each other for numbers one tool call returns. The one part where a separate role earned its keep was the customer-facing draft. A focused Customer Service prompt kept internal stuff (the unpaid invoice, the $160 balance) out of the letter.
    Evidence: Ticket 103: draft #2 to the Yale AI Club Ticket 101: draft #1 to Tauhid Zaman
  • Why: with three tickets, 24 rows of data and one cash account, the hard part isn’t expertise, it’s following the rules. Those rules live in the MCP tools, which every agent shares. Splitting into five agents mainly added coordination cost (≈128k tokens for the run) and new failure modes like the duplicate approval. The team design pays off when you need separation of duties (Customer Service literally can’t touch money, and no agent can approve), cleaner audit trails by role, and a board that’s easy for a person to follow. For a shop this size, I’d run one agent with all the read tools plus request-only money tools, and keep a separate draft-writer.

5. Three new problems this team could NOT solve, and what it would need

A. Receiving a delivery and actually filling the order

Why we can’t: no tool ever adds stock or ships anything. When Bulldog’s reprint arrives on 9/05, inventory still says tee S = 0, and nothing marks Tauhid’s order as fulfilled. We also have no orders table, so a sale never lowers stock.

What we’d need:

  • New MCP tools: receive_shipment(po_id, qty) (raises inventory.qty and closes the PO), create_order, reserve_stock, fulfill_order (lowers stock and records a shipment), plus purchase_orders and orders tables.
  • A Fulfillment / Receiving agent to match deliveries to open POs and waiting customers.
  • A human check-in step to confirm the box count.
Evidence: Ticket 101: Boss said no date can be promised, because nothing tracks the reprint arriving Ticket 103: 12 hoodies still “later”

B. Money coming in: sales, refunds and covering a shortfall

Why we can’t: cash only goes out in our model. The team can see that $160 won’t cover October rent or the $264 hoodie reprint, but it can’t record the Yale AI Club paying $320 for the 8 hoodies, issue a refund, or line up short-term credit.

What we’d need:

  • New MCP tools: record_sale / record_customer_payment (money in, tied to an order), issue_refund (human-approved), cash_forecast(days) (upcoming bills vs. expected receipts), and possibly request_credit_line_draw.
  • A Sales / Revenue agent and a Forecasting step in Accounting.
  • Tighter approvals: a refund, like a payment, must never run without a person.
Evidence: Cash: “What the cash can’t cover”, $264 restock vs. $160, no money in

C. Really talking to the outside world: sending messages, placing vendor orders, handling replies

Why we can’t: by design, everything stops at a draft. The team can’t email Tauhid, can’t send Bulldog the PO, and can’t read the landlord’s reply or a customer saying “actually make it size L.” New tickets also only appear if someone puts them in the table, so nothing turns an inbox into tickets.

What we’d need:

  • New tools: send_approved_message(draft_id) (only after a human approves the exact text), send_purchase_order(po_id) to the vendor’s email or portal, read_inbox → create_ticket, and track_shipment.
  • An Intake / Triage agent that turns emails into tickets and links replies to existing ones.
  • Guardrails: an allow-list of recipients, rate limits, PII checks, and a full send log in the audit trail.

Honorable mention: physical repairs (a broken window at the Chapel Street shop). Facilities can only read the lease. It would need a maintenance_requests table, a contractor vendor list, and a quote-and-approve tool.

Evidence: Ticket 101: draft #1 saved, not sent Ticket 103: draft #2 saved, not sent Ticket 102: the landlord’s email arrived as a ticket someone had to enter; Facilities checked it against the lease

Bottom line

The safety design worked: no agent could approve or pay, the tools enforced the business rules, and every dollar has a name on it. Next time I’d spend less on coordination. I’d tell agents to trust a teammate’s cited report instead of re-asking, give the Boss a discount ceiling (say 20%), and route simple tickets like rent to a single agent, keeping the team for tickets that cross departments.
